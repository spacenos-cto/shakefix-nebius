FOR APPS PEOPLE CAN'T AFFORD TO GET WRONG

One shake fixes it.

Margaret is 78 and takes five prescriptions. Her pharmacy app charges her $24 for a $12 copay. Reporting that today takes steps to reproduce, a screenshot, an app version and someone who believes her. So she calls her daughter, or she quietly stops using the app.

With ShakeFix she shakes her phone. That is the whole bug report. GitLab Duo agents reproduce it, fix it, prove the fix changed only what it should, and ship it. Then her phone asks: “Fixed ✓. Is it working for you now?”

    Judging criteria and the evidence

    Each row is one criterion: what we claim, and the link that proves it.

    Live proof: signed certificates

    Every closed case gets a certificate signed by the intake on Cloud Run (HMAC-SHA256, key in Secret Manager). This page fetches each one now and asks the intake's public endpoint to verify the signature. “Touches” is the certificate's own count, and we don't edit it.

    Limits, said plainly

    1. Few hands-off runs. Each certificate counts 1 touch. On #14 and #16 that touch was our own intake's note, miscounted by a webhook race we fixed afterwards; on #15 we rebased after breaking main; on #17 a person merged by design.
    2. CareRx is a demo app with synthetic data. Its four bugs are seeded, each modelled on a documented real failure.
    3. Reproduction needs her words. Taps show what she did; her one-line note says what was wrong. From taps alone, 3 of 10 seeded bugs reproduce.
    4. Triggers need a human token. GitLab flow triggers ignore bot users, so the intake files issues as the maintainer.
    5. What runs where. The four agents are one GitLab Duo flow. Second Opinion's optional labeller calls Claude from CI and can only make a verdict stricter.
